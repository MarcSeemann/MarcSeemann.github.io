Project 1 · PET scanners

CsI(Tl) crystal as a new candidate

I worked on PET scanner R&D, testing a new CsI(Tl) crystal as a possible candidate material. The advantage of CsI(Tl) is its cheaper price tag, compared to the commercially used LYSO crystals. The issue that CsI(Tl) brings is poorer performance at room temperature. Our research focused on the study of CsI(Tl) at cryogenic temperatures at which they become a significant option to consider, especially for larger full body PETs, which have a large fraction of their cost coming from the purchase of the LYSO crystals.

Initial testing included measurements at cryogenic temperatures and a Na-22 source to try and replicate the coincidence measurements done in PET scanners.

Related work: Cryogenic cesium iodide as a potential PET material (arXiv:2406.13598).

Project 2 · Gaseous xenon prototype

CIGAR

CIGAR is a prototype detector in which wavelength-shifting (WLS) fibers mounted on PTFE panels sit inside a gas vessel, read out by SiPMs, with a calibration LED and a Peltier cell. My responsibilities:

  • Hardware: fiber gluing and evaporation.
  • Data taking with the prototype.
  • Simulation (Geant4): a realistic muon generator and studies with Am-241 and Na-22 sources. See Simulation.

The goal of this prototype was proving the capability of the fibers working as an effective light collection system. The current problem that many neutrinoless double beta decay experiments face is the intrinsic radioactivity of the photosensors themselves. This can be avoided by using optical fibers for collecting the photons produced during physical processes and guiding them towards the photosensors, which can now be kept outside the active part of the detector.

Project 3 · LiquidO

COCOA prototype

Using the previous experience with the CsI(Tl) crystal, a new prototype COCOA was suggested that is meant as a Compton camera. It is a new approach to try and detect high energy gamma rays (at MeV energies) from astrophysical processes. It consists of the Sussex tile from the LiquidO collaboration, consisting of optical fibers running through an opaque scintillator medium, in combination with our CsI(Tl) crystals. The Sussex tile allows for positional reconstruction of scattering gamma rays, while the crystal is used as an absorber for energy reconstruction. I successfully managed to take initial results with a Cs-137 and a Co-60 source.

See the COCOA paper: arXiv:2502.20916.

Project 4 · Current

HD-DEMO

HD-DEMO is the new NEXT prototype detector, now being commissioned at DIPC. It builds on the fiber-barrel concept described in Towards a fiber barrel detector for next-generation high-pressure gaseous xenon TPCs, and was announced on the NEXT website. My contributions so far:

  • Fiber panels: gluing the fibers, attaching them to the panels and evaporating them.
  • Calibration: I built a calibration city, a program within IC (the NEXT software framework) that allows the fibers to be calibrated.
  • Simulation (latest work): contributing to the HD-DEMO simulation, used to understand the detector geometry further.
